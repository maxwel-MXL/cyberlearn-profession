Module 1: Cybersecurity Foundations

1. What is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting systems, networks, and digital information from cyber threats such as hacking, malware, phishing, and data breaches. It involves implementing technologies, processes, and policies designed to prevent unauthorized access, minimize risk, and ensure business continuity. In today’s digital world, cybersecurity is critical because almost every organization relies on technology to store sensitive data and operate securely.

🔐 The CIA Triad

  • Confidentiality – Data is accessible only to authorized users.
  • Integrity – Data cannot be altered without permission.
  • Availability – Systems and data remain accessible when needed.

2. Types of Cyber Threats

  • Malware (Viruses, Worms, Ransomware)
  • Phishing Attacks

    • Phishing is a social engineering attack where cybercriminals impersonate trusted organizations to trick users into revealing sensitive information such as passwords, credit card details, or personal data. Phishing often occurs through fake emails, SMS messages, or websites that appear legitimate but are designed to steal information. Organizations train employees regularly to identify suspicious links and verify sender authenticity to prevent phishing attacks.

  • Denial of Service (DoS)
  • Man-in-the-Middle (MITM)
  • Social Engineering

3. Practical Example

If a hacker sends a fake email pretending to be your bank and tricks you into entering your password, that is a Phishing Attack.

If ransomware encrypts your files and demands payment, it affects:

  • Confidentiality (data exposed)
  • Integrity (data altered)
  • Availability (data locked)

4. Certification Insight (Security+ / CEH)

Most entry-level cybersecurity certifications begin with:

  • Risk Management
  • Threat Identification
  • Basic Cryptography
  • Access Control

Understanding these foundations prepares you for certifications like:

  • CompTIA Security+
  • Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH)
  • ISC² CC

5. Mini Lab (Thinking Exercise)

Scenario: You run a small company website. What security measures would you implement to protect it?

  • Use HTTPS (SSL certificate)
  • Strong password policies
  • Firewall protection
  • Regular backups
